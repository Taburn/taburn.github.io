Component Tray

I wanted to start storing electronic components in uniform bags with uniform labels. In search of a way to store the bags, I couldn't find any shoebox sized containers that were the right size. I'd previously designed a folding carton [link here], so I decided to try making a basic cardboard box.

Here's what I came up with:

Finding Cardboard

I spent some time looking around for where I could buy cardboard in person in Calgary. It turned out the only place I could find that would let me buy single sheets of cardboard was Lee Valley (SKU 45K2072), where a 2' x 3' sheet is $2.

Design

I used the sketcher workbench in FreeCAD, since I wanted this to be a parametric design and it's the only program I know that can do 2D parametric drawings. I started by defining all the parameters:

Then I just tried drawing the design I had in mind, cut it out, and tried folding it. I ended up doing 7 versions until I had something I was happy with: the walls ended up straight, the end product matched the values of the parameters, and so on. Here's the final design.

I don't think it's perfect, but the problems are small enough I won't notice them.

It stays together with friction.

Creasing Tools

It turns out a good way to fold cardboard precisely is to crush it first. I ended up 3D printing two tools: a pointy one for 90° bends and a wider one for 180° bends.
